# 01 — Etsy vs Redbubble: the honest comparison

Verified against 2026 fee data. Sources listed at the bottom.

---

## Head-to-head

| Factor | **Etsy** | **Redbubble** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Marketplace; **you** set retail prices | Marketplace; Redbubble sets a base price, you add a markup % |
| Who fulfils the order | You, or a POD partner you connect (Printify, Printful, Gelato) | Redbubble — always. Fully hands-off. |
| Digital downloads | ✅ Yes, huge category | ❌ No — physical products only |
| Traffic | High, but **search-SEO driven**. You must rank. | ~16–25M monthly visitors. Design sits there and sells. |
| Listing fee | $0.20 per listing, renews every 4 months | $0 |
| Sale fee | 6.5% transaction (incl. shipping) | Taken via base price + platform fee |
| Payment processing | 3% + $0.25 (US); 4% + £0.20 (UK); 4% + €0.30 (EU); ~4.5% + flat (parts of Asia/S. America) | Included |
| Platform fee on your margin | none | **Standard 50% / Premium 20% / Pro 0%** — capped at $150 per payment period |
| Excess markup fee | none | 50% of any margin earned **above a 20% markup** (Standard & Premium) |
| Offsite ads | 15% if under $10k/yr (can opt out); **12% mandatory** over $10k/yr; capped $100/order | n/a |
| One-time setup cost | $15–$29 shop setup fee | $0 |
| Payout threshold | none (deposit schedule) | $20 minimum, rolls over until met |
| Effort per design | High: mockups, listing copy, SEO, customer service | Low: one upload, tick product boxes, done |
| Your margin on a tee | **$8.67** (free shipping) to **$13.19** (charging $5) | **$2.00** (Standard tier) |
| Your margin on a sticker | ~$2–4 | **~$0.11–0.50** |
| Control over brand | High — your own shop, banner, About page | Low — a profile inside their marketplace |
| Customer relationship | Yours (buyer sees your shop, can favourite it) | Redbubble's (you never get buyer details) |
| New-account friction | Shop setup fee, ID/bank verification, possible listing review | Standard tier by default; AI uploads limited to 5/day for new accounts |

## How each fee structure actually works

### Etsy — you set the price, they skim a percentage

```
You charge:            $24.99 item + $5.00 shipping = $29.99 order
Listing fee:           −$0.20
Transaction fee 6.5%:  −$1.95   (of $29.99 — note: shipping is included)
Processing 3% + $0.25: −$1.15   (of $29.99)
                       ────────
Etsy takes:            −$3.30   = 11.0% effective rate
You keep:               $26.69  before your product cost
```

Effective rate **falls as price rises** — the $0.20 and $0.25 flat fees hurt
cheap items disproportionately. A $2.99 item loses 24.6% to fees; a $34.99 item
loses 10.8%. **This is the single most important pricing insight on Etsy:
cheap listings are structurally unprofitable.**

Add an Offsite-Ads-attributed sale and the rate jumps to **~27%**.

### Redbubble — they set the cost, you add a markup

```
Classic T-Shirt, base price $20.00 (Redbubble's cost + their cut)
You set 20% markup  →  margin $4.00  →  retail $24.00

Standard tier: platform fee 50% of $4.00 = −$2.00
               YOU GET $2.00  (8.3% of retail)

Premium tier:  platform fee 20% of $4.00 = −$0.80
               YOU GET $3.20  (13.3% of retail)

Pro tier:      platform fee 0%
               YOU GET $4.00  (16.7% of retail)
```

### The excess-markup trap

Raising your markup above 20% triggers an extra 50% fee on everything above the
threshold. It *does* still earn you more — just far less than it appears:

```
Premium Hoodie, base $40.00, 30% markup (Standard tier)

Gross margin:                 $12.00
Excess markup fee:            −$2.00   (50% of the $4 above the 20% threshold)
Adjusted margin:               $10.00
Platform fee (50%):           −$5.00
                              ────────
YOU GET:                       $5.00   (9.6% of the $52 retail price)
```

Going 20% → 30% on that hoodie earned **$1.00 extra** while raising the retail
price by **$4.00** — which costs you conversion rate. The fee structure is
deliberately designed to keep you at 20%.

### Tier promotion is not something you can apply for

Redbubble assigns tiers using undisclosed criteria: "design quality, artistic
skill level, volume of designs, account activity, and commercial success." Pro
is an invitation-only top-seller programme reviewed annually. **Plan your
finances as if you will stay on Standard forever.**

## The verdict

### Run both. They are not competitors — they do different jobs.

**Etsy = your profit engine.**
Realistic margin is 3–6× Redbubble per sale. You own the customer relationship,
you control pricing, and digital downloads are near-pure profit. But it demands
work: 10 photos per listing, 13 tags, real SEO, and customer service. Nothing
sells until you rank.

**Redbubble = your free distribution channel.**
Upload once, tick the product boxes, and it sells while you sleep across 70+
product types to an audience you'd never reach otherwise. The per-unit economics
are terrible, but your marginal cost is also zero. Treat it as free lottery
tickets: most designs earn nothing, a few earn steadily for years.

### Where to put your effort

| If your design is... | Lead with | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Illustration / artwork / pattern | **Etsy digital download + wall art** | Highest margin, buyers search Etsy specifically for printable art |
| Funny text / meme / slogan | **Redbubble** (stickers + tees) | Impulse buys; Redbubble's audience browses for these |
| Niche hobby subject (birds, fishing, D&D, nursing) | **Both, Etsy first** | Etsy ranks well for specific long-tail searches |
| Photographic | **Etsy wall art / prints** | Redbubble's photo products underperform |
| Seasonal (Christmas, Halloween) | **Both, 2–3 months early** | Search volume spikes then dies |

### Worth adding later (same artwork, near-zero extra work)

| Platform | Why |
|---|---|
| **TeePublic** | Flat $4/tee for Artisans, **zero platform fees**, 15 tags. Better per-unit than Redbubble Standard. Note: Apprentice tier hides you from search until promoted. |
| **Society6** | Strong for wall art and home decor; design-conscious buyers. |
| **Displate** | Metal prints, high price point, curated audience. |
| **Amazon Merch on Demand** | Enormous traffic, but application/approval required and strict content rules. |
| **Printify/Printful → Etsy** | The POD backend that makes the Etsy physical-product model work. |

Every platform you add multiplies reach without new design work. Upload to all
of them from one master file — see `04-file-specs-and-upload.md`.
